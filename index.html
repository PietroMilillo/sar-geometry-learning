SAR Geometry

Two interactive exercises built on real Umbra X-band scenes and Copernicus 30 m topography. Nothing here is a cartoon: every angle is read from the acquisition's own metadata, and every distortion is computed from the terrain that was actually under the satellite.

EXERCISE 01

Look Angle

Eleven scenes. You are shown an amplitude image and asked one question — where was the satellite flying, where was it looking, which way does relief lean? You answer by drawing on the image, and you are marked against that scene's own sidecar.

  • Locate the ground track and the line of sight from metadata alone
  • Tell a squinted acquisition from a broadside one, and say why it matters
  • Read the three squint conventions without mixing them up

11 scenes · 8 targets · incidence 15–70° · squint −44° to +44°
both orbit directions, both look sides · ~20 min

EXERCISE 02

Where the Summit Lands

Twenty acquisitions over four targets, each with seven pixel-registered layers. A SAR image is a map of range, and the geocoding converts range to position by assuming the whole scene sits at one height. Click any point on the topography and see where the product actually puts it.

  • Explain why a GEC product is not a map, and predict the error
  • Derive and apply the layover and shadow thresholds
  • Compare one volcano across five look angles and account for the differences

20 acquisitions · Fuego, Nevado del Ruiz, Gamalama, Quimbaya
SAR · optical · DEM · hillshade · simulation · masks · displacement · ~35 min

The one equation both exercises are built around

A radar measures time, which gives slant range. For a point at ground range g and height h, seen at incidence θ: r = g·sinθ − h·cosθ. Height shortens the range. To turn range back into a map position the processor must assume a height, and it assumes one constant value h_ref for the whole scene. Subtract the two and the error falls out:

displacement = −(h − h_ref) · cot θ

Everything else — layover, shadow, foreshortening, why terrain correction exists — follows from that line. At Fuego's 17.5° acquisition it reaches 8.2 km.

Using these

They run in any modern browser with no login, no install and no VPN. Both work on a laptop; the second is easier with a reasonably large window. Nothing you draw or click is recorded or sent anywhere — there is no server behind these pages.

Instructors: the source, the data-preparation pipeline and the verification tests are in the GitHub repository. Metadata hooks lists every field used and the traps found along the way.
